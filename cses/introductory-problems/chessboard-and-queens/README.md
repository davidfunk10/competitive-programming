## Chessboard and Queens (CSES Intro)

For this complete search problem, I iterated through all possible combinations of 1-8 because each queen could be placed on different columns 1-8 for each row. After placing the 8 queens in seperate rows and columns, I iterated through every queen to see if all of them were on non-restricted squares, and then I iterated through all queens, pair by pair, to determine if any of the queens were on any of the other's diagnols, using lines y+x=b and y-x=b for the two diagnols. If any of the queens were in the same diagnol, that invalidated the current permutation, so I did not increment `ans`, whereas if all the queens were on non-restricted squares and they were not in each other's diagnols, then I did increase `ans` by 1 for that permuation. This problem helped me understand how to classify different diagnols using lines y+x and y-x for diagnols with a negative and positive slope, respectively. This works because the `b value` or y-intercept remains constant, and all unique diagnols have a unique y-intercept.
